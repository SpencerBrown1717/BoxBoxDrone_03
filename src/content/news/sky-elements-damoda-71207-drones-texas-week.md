Guinness World Records reported on October 6 that Sky Elements Drone Shows and Damoda America launched 71,207 drones during performances in Blue Ridge, Texas, between September 27 and 30. The organization recognized that total as the most multirotors or drones launched in a week, ahead of a 2026 mark of 55,939 set by Aerial Illuminations Drone Shows and UVify.

A second title came on September 28: the largest aerial display of a mythical creature formed by drones, a dragon built from 10,183 aircraft, ahead of a December 31, 2025, record of 2,247 set in Ras Al Khaimah. Guinness adjudicator Michael Empric was on site. He described it as one of the most challenging drone attempts he had overseen, citing the weather and the scale of about 10,000 drones flying several times a day. After a thunderstorm followed the first show on September 27, he said the teams completed five successful shows on the final night.

## How the week was counted

The two outlets describe the same total in slightly different ways, and both should be kept in view. Guinness said each show used about 10,200 drones, and that Damoda delivered the fleet in boxes that store, charge, and recover each aircraft to the same slot. RedShark News reported seven shows over three nights, made up of six 10,000-drone shows and one 11,000-drone show, and said Sky Elements calls the 11,000-drone performance the largest drone show yet flown in the United States. Rick Boss, the company's chief executive, said in a statement quoted by RedShark that shows of this size had until then happened in Asia and the Middle East, and that "we proved an American team can fly them here at home."

Damoda's staff from Shenzhen worked with the Sky Elements crew, Guinness said. RedShark noted that Damoda, a Shenzhen firm behind several of China's large certified shows, opened a North American operation in May 2025, and that Sky Elements has previously used Skybrush software and UVify aircraft on other shows. The record report does not name a flight-control stack for the Blue Ridge week, and this story does not infer one.

## A week-long mark, not a single-show record

The 71,207 figure is a total for the week, not the size of one formation. RedShark, writing about the Texas titles, said the largest single drone display on its books at the time was still 33,615, flown over Dujiangyan, China, in May 2026. That distinction matters. A week of launches and a single night's formation are different records.

## What to watch

The certified facts are the week-long launch count, the 10,183-drone dragon, and a schedule that continued after a storm. What neither report settles is how such fleets will hold formation in everyday wind, or who will supply the software the next time a team tries a five-figure show. Those are the questions for operators who want displays to fly themselves, not only to set a mark on a quiet farm.
