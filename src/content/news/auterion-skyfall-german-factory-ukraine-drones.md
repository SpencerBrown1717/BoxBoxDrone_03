On November 20, Auterion and the Ukrainian manufacturer SkyFall said they intend to form a joint venture, Skyfall x Auterion, to manufacture drones for Ukraine in a new factory in Germany. The announcement is an agreement to form the venture, not a claim that the line is already running. Auterion published the news itself. Breaking Defense reported it the same day.

The companies said the agreement was signed in Berlin during the ninth German-Ukrainian Economic Forum, in the presence of German Federal Minister for Economic Affairs and Energy Katherina Reiche, and that German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and Ukrainian Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi attended the forum. They placed the venture under the official "Build with Ukraine" defense cooperation framework.

## Software on someone else's airframe

Auterion describes itself as an international defense software company, with offices in Arlington, Virginia, and Munich. SkyFall is a Ukrainian technology and defense firm that makes several aircraft, including the Vampire bomber and Shrike FPV drones. The new line, Auterion said, will start with the Shrike FPV platform and later expand to SkyFall interceptors and middle- and deep-strike systems. "Every drone combines a SkyFall airframe with Auterion avionics and AuterionOS," the company wrote.

That split is the point for a software firm. The airframe comes from a manufacturer that has been building at wartime volume. The avionics and the operating system come from Auterion. The companies said they have already delivered more than 50,000 Shrike strike drones to Ukrainian forces together. Lorenz Meier, Auterion's founder and chief executive, put the same figure in a quoted line about "advanced terminal guidance and target detection." Those numbers are the companies' own.

Auterion also said a Shrike variant "ranked first among manufacturers in the Pentagon's Drone Dominance Program." That ranking is the company's account of a U.S. program. The announcement does not include a Pentagon citation.

## Why Germany

Auterion said a German factory "adds production capacity outside the range of air strikes, while maintaining a knowledge link and short innovation cycle available to Skyfall's engineers." Feedback from units at the front, it said, reaches those engineers every week and goes into the German line. The company also argued that German funding would reach the Ukrainian front, and that Germany would gain production jobs and, over the long term, a domestic source of drones already used in Ukraine.

Breaking Defense, citing Reuters and an industry official, reported that the German government would provide funding for the factory and for drone orders, first for Ukraine and then for German forces, in the range of hundreds of millions of euros. Auterion's own release does not state a figure.

## What to watch

The next facts to look for are a site, a start date, and the first aircraft off a German line. Until then, the story is a planned joint venture: Ukrainian airframes, Auterion software, and production moved out of range of air strikes. Whether the Pentagon ranking and the 50,000-drone figure are confirmed by a government source remains open.
